# Decisions & assumptions

A running log of what this tool is, what it deliberately is not, and the
assumptions behind every number it produces. Written during the build so the
scoring can be argued with rather than taken on faith. Most of the README's
"key decisions" and "known limitations" sections are condensed from here.

---

## 1. What problem this solves

**A promising new game concept is a mechanic that is already demonstrating
demand on the App Store but is not yet saturated, and that can be converted
into a short, fair, head-to-head skill contest — the only format Blitz can
monetize.**

The tool does not invent games. It ranks mechanics visible in App Store chart
data by how convertible they are into a Blitz tournament, and explains why.

Two consequences, both intentional:

- A mechanic with strong market pull but low convertibility (4X strategy, a
  merge sim with weeks of meta-progression) is **correctly rejected**, even
  though a generic "App Store trends" tool would surface it as a top
  opportunity. That rejection is the product.
- "New" means new *to Blitz's portfolio*, not new to the world. Blitz's edge
  is operating familiar mechanics as cash tournaments, so a long-established
  puzzle mechanic nobody has turned into 1v1 is a better output than a
  genuinely novel genre.

## 2. Scored unit: the mechanic, not the app

Apps are evidence; mechanics are what gets ranked. "Block-fit placement" is a
scoreable unit with several carrier apps behind it. An individual app is not —
ranking apps would produce a leaderboard, which Blitz can already read for
free on the App Store.

An app carries at most two mechanics. It counts fully toward its primary and
is recorded against its secondary with a flag, so hybrid-casual titles are
visible without inflating every mechanic they touch.

**Evidence tiers.** A mechanic needs **≥3 carriers** to be scored on demand
and saturation; below that, publisher concentration and carrier-age statistics
are meaningless. Mechanics with 1–2 carriers go to a **watchlist**: still
convertibility-scored, but with demand and saturation marked as insufficient
evidence. The tail is not discarded, because a single-carrier mechanic holding
a top-10 chart slot is exactly the low-saturation case the tool exists to find
(on the 2026-10-03 snapshot, `logic_deduction` sits at #1 free with two
carriers).

Concept pitches are generated by an LLM as a final step on top of the
top-ranked mechanics, so the output is readable without the scoring becoming
unmeasurable.

## 3. Where the data comes from

Free, keyless, public HTTP endpoints served by Apple. No market-intelligence
vendor, no API key, no SDK, no account — plain GET requests returning JSON.
Everything joins on Apple's numeric app ID (`trackId`).

| Endpoint | Answers | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `itunes.apple.com/{cc}/rss/{chart}/limit=N/genre=6014/json` | *What is ranked today?* | Primary. Genre-scoped to Games and the only source publishing top-grossing. Undocumented and unsupported, so treated as best-effort. |
| `rss.marketingtools.apple.com/api/v2/{cc}/apps/{chart}/N/apps.json` | Same, fallback | Apple's current feed, but it dropped genre filtering and publishes no grossing chart, so results must be filtered to games downstream. |
| `itunes.apple.com/lookup?id={app_id}&country=us` | *What is this app?* | Batched: comma-separated IDs, so ~700 apps cost ~7 requests. |
| `itunes.apple.com/{cc}/rss/customerreviews/id={app_id}/json` | *What do players say?* | 50 reviews per page, 10 pages max. |

**Who executes the requests:** locally, the scripts on demand; in production,
a GitHub Actions runner every morning at 06:17 UTC (an odd minute, because
scheduled jobs queued at :00 are the most delayed under load).

**Where the data lands:** JSONL files committed back into this repository. Git
is the storage layer and the version history — no database to provision, and
anyone cloning the repo gets the full dataset. Reviews are committed
deliberately (~36 MB): "a working application, easy to run locally" is a
grading criterion, and an app that needs a 30-minute scrape against an
unsupported endpoint before it renders anything does not meet it.

**The only paid dependency** is the Anthropic API. Every call caches by app ID
or mechanic ID, so re-runs cost nothing.

## 4. Scope: collection vs analysis

Deliberately different, because rank history cannot be backfilled but can be
ignored at analysis time for free.

**Collection scope (wide, running daily since the first commit):** 6
storefronts (US, GB, DE, FR, JP, BR) × 3 charts (top free, paid, grossing),
Games genre, top 100 each.

**Analysis scope (narrow):**

- **US only.** The only market where Blitz's cash games operate, and even
  there state-level restrictions apply. Ranking a mechanic on Japanese chart
  performance would be ranking an opportunity Blitz cannot sell.
- **top_free and top_grossing only.**
- **One snapshot, pinned to 2026-10-03.** See §5.

The extra storefronts cost ~12 HTTP requests a day on a machine that isn't
mine, so the cross-market work in §9 is provisioned rather than hypothetical.

### Why top_paid is collected but excluded from scoring

Premium games are structurally unconvertible to cash tournaments:

- **Monetization is opposed, not merely different.** A player paying upfront
  is explicitly buying out of ads and IAP. Blitz is deposit-driven.
- **Session structure.** The paid chart is dominated by campaign, sandbox and
  simulation titles played for hours across weeks. The convertibility bar is a
  1–3 minute seeded round with a comparable score.
- **It is an IP chart**, heavily skewed to franchises whose mechanics cannot
  be reused without the brand that sells them.
- **It barely moves.** Very low churn, so as a *trend* source — the premise of
  this tool — it contributes almost no signal.
- **Scale.** Paid download volume is a rounding error against free.

The counter-argument is real: top_paid reveals mechanics players value enough
to pay for unprompted, with no ad-pressure confound. That deserves a dedicated
study rather than a silent drop (§9), so collection continues.

### Also out of scope

Google Play (a second taxonomy and scraper for marginal gain); download and
revenue estimates (paid data); ad creative and UA intelligence (a different
product); building a game (the output is a ranked brief, not a prototype).

## 5. The universe is a single pinned snapshot

The analysis universe is the apps charting on **one** day, not the union of
all days collected.

**Why latest rather than union:** a mechanic that charted last week and does
not today is not an opportunity worth routing a designer toward. The tool
answers "what is live now"; historical snapshots serve momentum (§9) rather
than membership. It also keeps enrichment cost bounded and the dataset legible
— one universe, one `captured_at`, one set of claims.

**Why pinned rather than "newest on disk":** every pipeline stage originally
resolved "the latest chart" independently, and the daily Action moved that
target underneath them mid-build. The aggregation silently analysed a newer
chart's 162 apps using the previous day's 164 classifications. Three fixes
came out of it, and all three are in the code:

1. Every stage accepts `--chart-date` so a run can be pinned and reproduced.
2. The aggregation **reports coverage loudly** — apps in scope, apps fully
   covered, and the IDs of any that are missing metadata or a mechanic. A
   pipeline that quietly analyses 150 of 162 apps is worse than one that
   refuses to run.
3. Cached stages must be re-run when the universe shifts; all of them skip
   what they already have, so the daily refresh costs pennies.

## 6. Assumptions and known limitations

Each is a known weakness stated deliberately, not an oversight.

1. **Chart depth is 100, not 200.** Apple caps the legacy feed at 100 results
   despite accepting `limit=200`. Mechanics that only ever rank 100–200 are
   invisible.
2. **Review volume comes from `userRatingCount`, never from the review files.**
   Review pulls are capped at 500 per app and ~70% of in-scope apps hit that
   cap, so the time span covered varies from hours to weeks (Royal Match's 500
   reviews span 23 days; a small app's span years). Rates over the sample stay
   valid; counts and "reviews per day" computed from the files would be
   measuring the cap, not the market.
3. **Voice signals need ≥50 reviews.** Below that, a rate is noise — 4 of 21
   reviews is numerically 19% but nothing like as trustworthy as 95 of 500.
   Thin apps are marked insufficient rather than scored, and mechanic-level
   rates pool reviews across all carriers so a thin app contributes without
   dominating.
4. **Average review sentiment is not used as a feature.** Reviews are pulled
   most-recent-first and unhappy players review more promptly, so the sample
   skews negative in a way that varies by app. Specific signals (competitive
   intent, fairness complaints, ad fatigue) are robust to that skew; aggregate
   sentiment is not. This is why the review pass looks for named signals
   rather than a mood score.
5. **Mean star rating is reported but not scored.** The observed range across
   44 mechanics is 4.0–4.93, with nearly everything between 4.6 and 4.9 —
   App Store ratings are compressed by prompt-gating. The "high rank +
   mediocre rating = unmet demand" heuristic has no signal to detect here, so
   unmet demand is taken from review text instead.
6. **Reviews in the US storefront are multilingual.** Spanish appears
   frequently. Signals are therefore classified by LLM rather than by English
   keyword matching, which would silently undercount.
7. **The reviews feed intermittently returns a valid but empty response under
   load**, indistinguishable from an app genuinely having no reviews. Six of
   164 in-scope apps were affected on the first full run, including 8 Ball
   Pool (4.8M ratings) — a known-positive that would have been lost. Mitigated
   by retrying with a pause and an alternate sort order, by only retrying when
   the previous page was full (a genuinely exhausting feed returns a partial
   page first), and by never caching an empty result. Roughly 30 recoveries
   fired on the full run. An app failing every retry still contributes no
   voice signal.
8. **Chart rank is a demand proxy, not a demand measure.** Rank is ordinal and
   non-linear — the gap between #1 and #5 dwarfs #50 to #55. Scores use
   rank-derived features, never raw rank arithmetic.
9. **The taxonomy is hand-seeded and was corrected twice by its own failures.**
   It began at 38 mechanics seeded from the live US top 100. The classifier's
   `other` bucket then exposed real gaps — location-based AR, tap-to-blast
   (distinct from match-3 swap), poker, precision aim, jigsaw, dress-up —
   taking it to 46. Three apps still resist classification and are handled by
   `data/overrides.json`, applied visibly and stamped `human_override` rather
   than blended in silently.
10. **Convertibility is judged by an LLM from store descriptions**, not from
    playing the games. It will be wrong about round length and seeding in
    individual cases. Every score carries a justification, a supporting app
    ID, and a confidence derived from run-to-run variance (§8).
11. **Momentum features are defined but not populated.** The deliverable runs
    on a single day, so rank deltas do not exist yet. The daily Action has
    been collecting since the first commit, so this is a matter of elapsed
    time rather than missing design (§9).
12. **No legal advice.** Gambling-adjacency and US state restrictions are
    flagged as risks for a human to check, nothing more.

## 7. Technical decisions

- **Stdlib only, including the API client.** Nothing to install; the whole
  project runs on a bare Python 3 interpreter. "Easy to run locally" is a
  grading criterion, and `pip install` is a step that can fail on someone
  else's machine. The retry/backoff logic the SDK would provide already lives
  in `scripts/common.py`.
- **Raw snapshots are immutable.** Fetch, normalise, store. All scoring
  recomputes from raw JSONL, so changing a weight never re-fetches.
- **Idempotent by day.** Re-running the collector replaces that day's file
  rather than appending duplicates.
- **Partial failure is not job failure.** One storefront timing out is normal;
  the collector exits non-zero only when every feed fails — the signal that
  Apple's APIs moved.
- **Dual-source with fallback**, and the source that answered is recorded on
  every row.
- **Model routing: cheap on volume, expensive on judgement.** Haiku 4.5 for
  review classification (~95% of token volume, near-zero judgement), Sonnet
  5.5 for mechanic extraction, Opus 5.5 for the convertibility rubric and the
  concept pitches. Using one model everywhere would either overpay on volume
  or underperform on the scoring that the product rests on.
- **The taxonomy and rubric are cached prompt prefixes.** ~1.9k tokens,
  identical on every call. Measured on the full classification run: 76,380
  tokens read from cache against 3,819 written. 164 apps classified for $0.29.
- **Scoring criteria live in `data/rubric.json`, not inside a prompt string**,
  so they can be reviewed, versioned and argued with as an artifact.
- **Invented mechanic IDs are coerced to `other` and logged**, never silently
  accepted. A model inventing a category is a taxonomy gap for a human to
  review, not a new class appearing in the data unannounced.
- **The cash-competitor flag is derived deterministically**, not classified:
  either mechanic slot being `skill_cash_arcade`, or a title matching
  "win real cash/money". Auditable, free, and the rule can be read.
- **`data/_runs.jsonl`** records per-feed status, row counts and errors for
  every collector execution, and is union-merged via `.gitattributes` so the
  daily Action's append never conflicts with local work.
- **macOS note:** python.org builds ship without a certificate bundle; run
  `Install Certificates.command` or every HTTPS call fails with
  `CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_FAILED`.

## 8. Scoring design

**Two gates, six weighted dimensions.** `symmetric_seeding` and
`score_comparability` are gates: scoring at or below their threshold rejects
the mechanic outright and names the failing gate. They are not averaged in,
because a mechanic that cannot be run fairly is not partially convertible.
The six weighted dimensions sum to 1.0, with `skill_expression` highest at
0.25 — it is what makes the product skill gaming rather than gambling.

**Symmetric randomness, not determinism.** The criterion is whether both
players can face an *identical randomised instance* — same seed, same deal,
same draw — so variance cancels and the score gap reflects skill alone. This
is why Blitz can operate Solitaire and Bingo, which contain randomness, and
why poker fails: two opponents cannot be dealt the same hand, and the
asymmetry is the game. "Deterministic vs random" would have rejected half of
Blitz's actual portfolio.

**Gambling adjacency is scored on its own axis**, not folded into
convertibility. It is a brand and compliance risk rather than a design
property: a mechanic can be provably skill-based and still read as a casino
product to a platform reviewer, and Blitz positions explicitly away from a
"win big" pitch.

**Two mechanics are excluded from the scored set**, both visibly rather than
silently: `skill_cash_arcade` (not a gameplay loop — these are cash-tournament
wrappers around other people's mechanics, i.e. competitors) and `casino_slots`
(fails both gates, reported as REJECTED so the rejection is on the record).

**Competitor carriers are counted separately from ordinary carriers.** Same
data, opposite reading: an ordinary carrier is evidence of saturation, a cash
carrier is evidence the conversion *works*. Blended together, Mahjong looks
crowded; split, it reads as proven demand with two competitors already live.
This produces four quadrants — already operated, proven by competitor,
unproven established, unproven emerging — which is more useful to a designer
than a single ranked list.

### Evaluation

Scoring that cannot be checked is an opinion with a number attached.

- **Known positives.** Blitz operates or builds on bingo, solitaire, cue
  sports, match-3, block-fit, physics demolition and territory capture. Those
  must land in the top decile or the weights are wrong. The list lives in
  `rubric.json` and does double duty as the "already operated" quadrant.
- **Known negatives.** Survival 4X, gacha RPG and long-horizon merge must
  score low on convertibility despite high demand.
- **Agreement with human judgement.** Spearman correlation against ~25
  hand-labelled mechanics.
- **Self-consistency.** Three runs per mechanic, variance reported in
  aggregate and surfaced per mechanic as a confidence badge. Note that Sonnet
  5.5 and Opus 5.5 no longer accept a `temperature` parameter, so this
  measures genuine judgement stability rather than decoder determinism —
  which is closer to what the metric is actually for.
- **Groundedness.** Every claim carries an app ID or review ID; unsourced
  claims are counted and reported.
- **Cost and latency** per full run, printed by each script.

## 9. What a further week would buy

Ordered by value. The first two need *elapsed time*, which is why collection
started on day one rather than when the analysis was written.

1. **Momentum from accumulated snapshots.** Every demand feature today is a
   static photograph. Joining all `data/charts/*.jsonl` against the existing
   assignments turns "sort puzzles are big" into "sort puzzles gained 14 chart
   positions in 7 days while match-3 lost 3". ~40 lines, no new API spend, and
   the data is already accruing.
2. **Cross-market lead/lag.** Six storefronts are being collected. Does a
   mechanic break in BR or JP before the US, and by how long? A confirmed
   leading indicator would outweigh anything in the current score.
3. **Backtesting.** With 3–4 weeks of history, score the charts as they stood
   at T−3 weeks and check whether the mechanics the tool favoured actually
   rose. The only route to weights that are defensible rather than asserted.
4. **A dedicated top_paid study** (§4): which mechanics do players pay for
   unprompted, with no ad-pressure confound, and do any clear the
   convertibility bar? The data is already collected.
5. **Screenshot vision analysis** for convertibility, instead of relying on
   marketing copy that systematically overstates depth.
6. **Google Play cross-check**, to separate genuine mechanic trends from
   iOS-specific chart artefacts.
7. **State-by-state legality mapping** attached to each recommendation.

## 10. Open questions

- Weighting across dimensions is a judgement call, exposed as sliders rather
  than hidden. A principled default needs Blitz's own portfolio performance.
- Whether two mechanics per app is too tight for hybrid-casual titles, which
  deliberately stack loops.
- Whether `logic_deduction` at #1 free with two carriers is a genuine
  breakout or a chart artefact — the watchlist tier exists precisely because
  the tool cannot yet tell.
